# Vibroxi website strategy

## Commercial position

Vibroxi is positioned as a specialist commercial finishing and property-maintenance execution contractor. The website is intended to attract larger project scopes, repeat work and subcontracting relationships—not small household repair enquiries.

## Priority buyers

1. Facility management companies and integrated facilities providers that need specialist finishing subcontractors.
2. Commercial property owners, asset managers and operators.
3. Hotels and hospitality groups.
4. Corporate offices, business parks and multi-site occupiers.
5. Industrial and institutional property operators, subject to the team's actual technical capability and safety requirements.
6. Commercial fit-out contractors needing painting, polishing and finishing execution (not design or construction).

## Core service scope

Publish only services Vibroxi can actually deliver and supervise:
- Commercial interior and exterior painting / repainting.
- Wood polishing and refinishing.
- Surface preparation and finishing.
- Electrical installation and maintenance only after capability, licensing and safety requirements are verified.
- Planned property maintenance and recurring work packages where staffing and response capacity support the commitment.

Do not position Vibroxi as an interior design studio, construction contractor, turnkey builder or general household handyman marketplace. Do not claim waterproofing, industrial coatings, specialist electrical certifications, PU/Duco systems or other specialist work unless confirmed by the operating team.

## Contract strategy

The site should support three buying routes:
- Direct project contracts: defined commercial property scopes, refurbishment and repainting.
- Specialist subcontracting: work packages for facility-management providers and commercial fit-out contractors.
- Recurring maintenance: periodic inspections and planned finishing/maintenance scopes, with agreed service levels and exclusions.

Do not claim an AMC response time, nationwide mobilisation, workforce size, insurance, certifications or vendor approvals until verified.

## SEO and content principles

- One primary search intent per canonical URL.
- Build useful service, buyer/industry and location clusters; avoid thin city doorway pages.
- Every indexable page must have a unique title, description, H1 and useful body content.
- Keep primary content server-rendered in the initial HTML.
- Use contextual internal links between service, industry, project, delivery and location pages.
- Include only real projects, approved photographs, verified testimonials and substantiated credentials.
- Do not publish placeholder case studies as if they were completed projects.
- Do not add FAQ or review structured data for content that is not visibly present and accurate.
- Sitemap entries must match canonical, indexable routes; do not assign the current runtime date as every page's modification date.
- Preserve existing URLs where possible. Add redirects only for deliberate URL changes after confirming the production domain and current indexed paths.

## Competitor research principles

Use large brands and platforms for page architecture, buying journeys, proof presentation and service explanations. Use local specialists to understand service-specific search intent. Do not copy competitor wording, claims, imagery or customer evidence.

## Launch prerequisites

- Confirm the production domain and set NEXT_PUBLIC_SITE_URL.
- Replace all placeholder phone/email/company details.
- Connect and test the enquiry form endpoint and file upload handling.
- Verify the exact service scope, qualifications, safety process and geographic capacity.
- Add real project photography and approved case studies.
- Test redirects, metadata, sitemap, robots, canonical URLs and production build.
